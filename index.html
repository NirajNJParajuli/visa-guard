The trap most students fall into

A "fortnight" is any 14 days starting on a Monday. A new one starts every week. You must be at or under 48 hours in every one of them.

✓ Weeks 1 + 2 = 48 hours. Fine.
✗ Weeks 2 + 3 = 58 hours. Over by 10.

If you only count "every second week," you'd never notice. Add a second job and it gets harder. Home Affairs uses the same kind of example on its own condition 8105 page: 15, 30, 30 and 10 hours is a breach in weeks 2 + 3.

    Questions

    What exactly is the rule?

    Student visa holders (subclass 500) with work condition 8105 may work up to 48 hours per fortnight while their course is in session, and unlimited hours when it is not (for example, during scheduled course breaks). Home Affairs says "a fortnight is a period of 14 days starting on a Monday," so a new fortnight starts every Monday and they overlap. Check your own conditions in VEVO, since some visas have different conditions.

    What's the difference between Official and Strict mode?

    Official mode checks every 14-day period starting on a Monday, matching the regulation's wording. Strict mode checks every possible 14-day period. It may warn you more often, but it never misses one.

    What counts as "work"?

    According to Home Affairs, you've worked if you were at a workplace on a roster or timesheet (not counting unpaid breaks), were "clocked on" to an electronic system that records work, or were paid for the time according to your payslip. For rideshare and delivery drivers, time signed in and ready to accept jobs counts, not just time on trips. All jobs are added together.

    Does unpaid work or work experience count?

    Yes. Home Affairs says work experience counts toward the 48 hours whether it's paid or unpaid. The exception is a work placement that is a mandatory, registered part of your course (in its CRICOS registration). Students whose Master's by research or PhD has started can also work more than 48 hours. If you're unsure, check with your education provider or a registered migration agent.

    What if my employer pressures me to work more?

    You don't have to accept. If you're being exploited at work, the Fair Work Ombudsman can help, and Home Affairs has protections for visa holders who report workplace exploitation. See Fair Work: visa holders.
